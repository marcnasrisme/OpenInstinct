# Rivals beat Instinct on trust, not reach

As of November 2026, no agent beats Instinct on raw reach. It is still the only product that combines iMessage, SMS and WhatsApp, calls in both directions, its own phone number, computer and email address, payment rails and group chats in one service. On the governance checklist, though, Instinct scores at or near the bottom. It does not name its model provider. It trains on user data by default. It publishes no retention schedule and holds no security certification. In August 2026 named testers documented an email it sent without approval, inbox data it kept after Google was disconnected, and a successful prompt injection. The closest functional match is **Tab**, a private-beta iMessage/WhatsApp agent with calling, its own browser and wallet, an encrypted credential vault and approval gates. Among big labs, **Meta Muse** comes closest. The **most transparent** options are self-hosted: **Hermes Agent**, or a small agent you write yourself against the Anthropic or OpenAI API. Among hosted texting agents, **folk** is the most transparent. Weighing governance alongside capability, **OpenAI dots** is the strongest hosted agent overall if $100/month is acceptable, and **Claude with Cowork** is the best value at $20. So yes, several agents beat Instinct once transparency counts, but none does so by matching its reach. For a Mac-using Python learner, the best long-term path is a minimal agent of a few hundred lines that you own, with Hermes as a reference design. OpenClaw has improved a lot since February, but it is still a poor fit. Much of the evidence below is weeks old, vendor-sourced or relayed secondhand, and each section flags where.

## Instinct leads on ten reach items and fails most governance rows

Instinct, from Spear Street Technology, is a cloud agent you text or call, and it "performs tasks using its own phone number and computer" ([TechCrunch, Sept 28](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/28/viral-ai-agent-instinct-raises-1b-series-c-at-a-10b-valuation)). Hands-on users reach it over iMessage, WhatsApp and phone calls ([mager.co](https://mager.co/blog/2026-09-12-instinct/)). The company's own legal documents say only "phone or text," which suggests iMessage runs through a phone-number bridge rather than an Apple-sanctioned integration. Since September it has had **its own email address** at mail.instinct.com, which it uses to sign up for services, contact businesses and follow up "autonomously" ([TechCrunch, Sept 9](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/viral-ai-assistant-instinct-now-has-its-own-email-address/)). It has used **Stripe for purchases since August**, and outbound calls to businesses ("Concierge") have been in **early access since mid-September** ([TechCrunch, Sept 17](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/rival-ai-agents-instinct-and-metas-muse-both-add-the-ability-to-make-calls/)). Group-chat agents launched in early access on Oct 5 ([TechCrunch, Oct 5](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3173877)). Google Workspace is the only integration the privacy policy names, and Microsoft 365 is undocumented ([Instinct privacy policy](https://www.instinct.com/privacy-policy)).

The governance column reads very differently. The privacy policy says data goes to unnamed "third-party AI model providers." Training is **on by default**, the opt-out applies only "on a go-forward basis," and data flagged for safety review can still be used for training. The policy gives **no retention periods**. Disconnecting an integration "does not automatically delete data." The policy concedes data "may not be secure while in transit," and lists no SOC 2 or similar certification ([Instinct privacy policy](https://www.instinct.com/privacy-policy)). The terms warn that "Actions may not always be reversible" and cap liability at $100 or six months of fees ([Instinct terms](https://www.instinct.com/terms)).

The track record matches the policy. Katie Jacobs Stanton reported that Instinct sent an email without checking with her first. Alex Cohen got it to obey instructions emailed in from a fresh Gmail account, which is a live prompt injection. Claire Vo disconnected Google and still received an email summary three hours later; the bot said her emails were stored in plain text. Peter Yang could not get their Gmail records deleted until the team shipped a deletion tool ([TechCrunch, Aug 24](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3155931)). The worst license language ("perpetual and irrevocable") was removed in the Aug 26 revision, but training defaults and retention barely moved. Instinct has since raised **$1B at a $10B valuation**, is still **invite-only and free**, and is gated by available compute ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/30/noah-shinn-instinct-ai-assistant-meta-muse-alexandr-wang-tech-series-c-ai-agent-mark-zuckerberg/)). You might not be able to get in at all.

**Tab** is the closest match. Its site advertises iMessage, WhatsApp and group chats, booking restaurants and flights, and an agent that "makes calls and waits on hold." Flights, shopping, tickets and emails go out only after you approve them ([tab.bot](https://tab.bot/)). Its privacy policy is the most technically specific of any startup reviewed. Stored website credentials are encrypted with AES-256-GCM and bound to a user, origin and account label. The planning model sees only an opaque reference, never the password. The card sits in an isolated vault, and the app keeps only the brand and last four digits ([Tab privacy policy](https://tab.bot/privacy)). The caveats are large. Tab came out of stealth on Oct 6–7, its wallet and checkout are "limited beta features," it has no published price, and no independent hands-on exists.

**Wajo Fo** comes second. It has the strongest identity features of any rival: its own email address, phone number and payment card, plus a human who steps in when the agent gets stuck ([TechCrunch, Oct 24](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/24/all-the-ai-agents-that-can-live-in-your-text-messages/)). Its claim of 71% task completion versus 42% for OpenClaw is Wajo's own benchmark and has not been reproduced ([Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/wajos-fo-agent-can-call-email-and-pay-on-its-own-beating-rivals-at-getting-things-done/)). **szn** covers the same ground on paper, with its own name, email and phone number, purchases and vendor calls, but how it connects to iMessage is unclear and it is only weeks old ([MacDailyNews](https://macdailynews.com/2026/09/28/former-siri-engineer-launches-imessage-ai-agent-szn-that-can-shop-talk-to-vendors-and-act-on-your-behalf/)).

Among the big labs, **Meta Muse** is closest. It runs on WhatsApp in the US, gives each agent its own cloud VM, and connects to email, calendar, payments and shopping, with free, $20 and $100 tiers ([Reuters via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/metas-muse-arrives-three-pricing-183437528.html)). It added calling the same week as Instinct ([TechCrunch, Sept 17](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/rival-ai-agents-instinct-and-metas-muse-both-add-the-ability-to-make-calls/)). **OpenAI dots** gives each dot an isolated Linux machine with Chrome that you can watch and take over, and you can voice-call it. However, it **cannot call out, has no email address of its own**, and its texting is a US Pro-only beta ([eesel.ai summary of OpenAI help pages](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/openai-dots)). **Claude Cowork** runs tasks in the cloud on a schedule and can drive your real logged-in Chrome. Its only remote channel is the Claude mobile app, and no source shows it working over SMS, iMessage or phone ([Claude Help](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13947068-assign-tasks-to-claude-from-anywhere-in-cowork)). **Gemini Tasks** cannot make phone calls or complete purchases ([DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/gemini-spark)). Musk announced on Oct 25 that **Grok Bot** can now phone businesses, but no hands-on test of that has been published ([Tesla North](https://teslanorth.com/2026/10/25/grok-bot-phone-calls-elon-musk/)).

One result is easy to miss: self-hosted **OpenClaw** ties Tab for the second-highest reach score. It ships iMessage, WhatsApp and Signal channels plus an official Twilio/Telnyx voice-call plugin that places outbound calls ([OpenClaw docs](https://docs.openclaw.ai/plugins/voice-call); [GitHub](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw)). The catch is that you have to assemble and secure all of it yourself.

**dots** has the most complete approval system in the market. A separate auto-review layer checks each planned action against your rules, and the dot cannot turn it off. Custom Rules run from "act without asking" to "hand off." Password changes and money transfers always go back to the user, and approving one message does not grant ongoing permission to contact that person ([eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/openai-dots)). Its weaknesses are price (it starts at the **$100/month Pro tier**), no stated retention period, and memory that can only be cleared by deleting the whole dot. Most of these details come through eesel, a competing vendor, because OpenAI's help pages returned errors to the research tools.

**Anthropic publishes the most precise retention numbers** of any lab. Deleted consumer chats are purged from the back end within 30 days, safety-flagged content is kept up to 2 years, and training-enabled data is kept up to 5 years ([Anthropic Privacy Center](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/10023548-how-long-do-you-store-my-data)). Cowork offers Manual, Auto and Skip permission modes. Consumer plans default to Auto, which screens actions for prompt injection, and permanent file deletion always requires an explicit "Allow" ([Claude Help](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13345190-get-started-with-cowork)). **Google's agent is the least private by design**: Tasks in Gemini only works with Keep Activity on, which means 18-month default retention, eligibility for training, and human-reviewed copies kept up to 3 years ([Gemini Apps Privacy Hub](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961)).

Among the startups, **folk is the outlier on transparency**. It names its whole model chain: OpenRouter routing to Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and others, plus OpenAI for call audio. It says it never trains on user data, and it offers self-serve deletion of memories, messages and saved credentials ([folk privacy policy](https://www.folk.com/privacy)). Poke trains by default unless you choose "Maximum Privacy," and its policy does not name its new owner, Cognition ([Poke privacy policy](https://poke.com/privacy)). Wajo trains on personal data with no specific opt-out ([Wajo privacy policy](https://wajo.ai/privacy)).

Two gaps hold across the entire market. **No Instinct-like hosted agent cites a SOC 2 report**, and **no product reviewed ships a real user-facing audit log**; the closest are Tab's receipts and dots' Activity View.

Self-hosted agents win on transparency almost automatically. Hermes states that it "does not collect telemetry, usage data, or analytics," sends API calls only to the provider you configure, and keeps everything in `~/.hermes/` ([Hermes FAQ](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/reference/faq)). Pointed directly at a model API, your data sits on your own machine plus **up to 30 days at Anthropic or OpenAI, with no training by default** ([Anthropic Privacy Center](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996866-how-long-do-you-store-personal-data); [OpenAI "Your data"](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data)).

## The evidence is too thin to name a capability winner

The only head-to-head test of consumer agents is **Assistant Benchmark**. It scores Muse 9.3, Grok Bot 8.8, Instinct 8.7, dots 8.6, Wajo Fo 7.8, Poke 7.0 and folk 6.8 out of 10 ([Assistant Benchmark](https://assistantbenchmark.com/)). It does not explain its methodology or funding, and Muse was tested on **only 8 of 15 dimensions**, skipping proactivity, memory and phone calls ([Assistant Benchmark: Muse](https://assistantbenchmark.com/agents/muse)). Muse's lead is therefore partly an artifact of which tests it took. The best independent hands-on, The Verge's two-week test of Instinct, found 4 clean wins, 1 partial and 1 miss. It concluded Instinct "has held its own" against Muse and dots and was the least intrusive of the three. That review was read only through a secondary summary ([GenAI Magazine summary](https://thegenaimagazine.com/muse-vs-grok-bot-vs-instinct-ai-agent-comparison-verge-hands-on)). Across all of these products, failures came mostly from login walls and missing context, not from weak models. Muse stalled on Airbnb and Expedia ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/i-asked-muse-to-help-me-book-my-holiday-travel-it-didnt-fly-far-2000823264)), and Gemini's agent was blocked from signing in to Instacart ([Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/googles-new-gemini-spark-ai-agent-can-run-your-errands-while-you-run-your-life/)).

At the model layer, Claude models lead the official computer-use boards. Claude Opus 5.5 posts **81.8% partial / 48.7% binary** on the long-workflow OSWorld 2.0, but that score is self-reported. Settings alone can swing the same model's binary score from 18.8% to 44.3% ([Presenc AI compilation](https://presenc.ai/research/osworld-computer-use-leaderboard-2026)). Published prompt-injection numbers are vendor-authored and not comparable across labs. Anthropic reports a 31.5% attack success rate without safeguards falling to 0.5% with them, while Google publishes no figure at all ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/security/anthropic-browser-agent-hijacked-31-percent-before-safeguards-engaged)). The one cross-vendor academic study, from the University of Washington, found preconditions for cross-origin data theft in Chrome with Gemini, Claude for Chrome and Comet, and a full proof of concept on ChatGPT Atlas. Its authors "particularly caution" against Claude for Chrome's JavaScript capability ([UW Agent Security](https://agent-security.cs.washington.edu/agentic_browsers_sop.html)).

Treat every capability claim for Tab, Fo and szn as **vendor-sourced**. Treat the dots details as **secondhand from a conflicted source**, and the OpenClaw and Hermes CVE totals as **compiled by companies that sell competing or security products**.

## OpenClaw has grown up, but a small Python agent is the better build

If your February verdict on OpenClaw was "badly engineered," you caught it at its worst month. That was the period of the one-click RCE (CVE-2026-25253) and the ClawHavoc campaign, which grew from 341 malicious ClawHub skills to 1,400+, mostly macOS infostealers ([IBM X-Force](https://www.ibm.com/think/x-force/what-openclaw-reveals-about-agentic-ai-security-risks)). Since then, a nonprofit foundation employs the core team and signs releases ([GitHub](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw)). The project now ships stable, extended-stable (LTS-equivalent) and beta channels with linked validation runs ([GitHub releases](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw/releases)). Version 2.0 (Aug 30) added approvals bound to the exact command and person, plus per-session read-only and guarded modes ([CellCog](https://cellcog.ai/blog/openclaw-2-0/), a competitor source).

Real problems remain. Release notes still show skipped checks. The codebase has 124k commits and about 4.9k open issues. A competitor-cited tracker counts **543 CVEs** as of September ([BetterClaw](https://www.betterclaw.io/blog/openclaw-security-2026)). A large, fast-moving TypeScript codebase is exactly the kind you'd struggle to audit, which makes OpenClaw the wrong choice for someone who values engineering they can read.

**Hermes Agent** is the better open-source reference: MIT-licensed, written in Python, with a one-line macOS installer, cron delivery to any chat platform, and SQLite/FTS5 memory ([GitHub](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent)). Its security story is honest but sobering. Its own policy says "the only security boundary against an adversarial model is the operating system" ([NeuralTrust](https://neuraltrust.ai/blog/hermes-agent-security), a security vendor). An independent audit of v0.8 found 4 critical issues in the default configuration ([GitHub issue #7826](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/issues/7826)), and one advisory notes that the vendor did not respond to disclosure ([GitHub Advisory](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-pgp4-xr4j-h5cg)). If you run it, it belongs in Docker or on a separate VPS, not on your main Mac.

The **build-your-own** path fits you best. It combines:

- a Telegram bot;
- the Claude Agent SDK or OpenAI Agents SDK, with MCP servers for Gmail and Calendar;
- APScheduler for proactive messages;
- one SQLite file for memory and a real audit log;
- a "yes/no" prompt in Telegram before any send, delete or purchase.

The research notes estimate **300–800 lines of Python** for this. That is an unsourced estimate, but plausible. It fixes the exact failures Instinct had, because approval and logging are code you write and can read.

Outbound calls can come later. Vapi charges **$0.05/min** on top of telephony ([Vapi](https://support.vapi.ai/t/18904396/per-minute-cost-the-same-on-inbound-and-outbound-calls)), and a Twilio number costs about **$1–2/month** at roughly **$0.013/min** US outbound ([edesy](https://edesy.in/blog/twilio-voice-pricing-guide-2026)). What you give up compared with Instinct is iMessage (it needs an always-on Mac and an unofficial bridge), turnkey business calling, and a hardened logged-in browser.

So, is anything better than Instinct overall? On capability, no: Instinct and Muse are co-leaders, and the data cannot separate them. Once the user's criteria are weighted in, yes. dots, Claude, folk and every self-hosted option beat Instinct on model disclosure, training defaults and approvals, and Instinct's August record shows these are not abstract risks.

One disclosure: this report was written by Claude, an Anthropic model. The Cowork assessment rests on Anthropic's own help and privacy pages, so weigh that pick with this in mind.

## Conclusion

Instinct's documented failures were **control-layer failures**: no confirmation gate, and no deletion when an account was disconnected. A better model would not have prevented them. Its real moat is plumbing: a phone number, an email identity, payment rails and messaging bridges. That plumbing is what rivals lack, and it is also the part a student can partly rebuild with Twilio, a dedicated Gmail account and Telegram.

The features the market most lacks are a real audit log and enforced approvals. These are also the cheapest features to write yourself, which turns the build-your-own route from a hobby into the most rigorous option available. The signals to watch over the next few months are whether Instinct publishes a retention schedule and a model provider when it introduces paid tiers, and whether Tab or Fo get independent hands-on reviews. Until then, any capability ranking below the top two rests on marketing pages.
